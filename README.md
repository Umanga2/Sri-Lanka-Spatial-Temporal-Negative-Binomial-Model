Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) is a highly contagious transboundary animal disease that requires strategic control measures implemented at a national scale. Zoning, which involves dividing a country based on differing levels of FMD risk, plays a crucial role in disease control. FMD is endemic to Sri Lanka. For the first time, using a combination of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT) analysis, expert opinions, outbreak trend investigations, and spatial-temporal Bayesian modeling, we propose distinct FMD risk zones across the country. The results indicated high-risk zones in the North, Eastern, North-Central, and Western provinces. Possible control zoning boundaries to demarcate these areas were identified in focus group discussions conducted with veterinarians.
